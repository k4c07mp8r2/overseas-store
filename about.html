Our story

A studio, six workshops, one slow idea.

We started because we could not find objects that were made honestly, priced honestly, and built to outlast a season. So we began making them.

The studio

Where it started

Two rooms and one kiln.

The first batch was forty carafes. Thirty-one came out of the kiln well enough to sell; the rest became the water jugs in our own kitchen. That ratio is roughly how it still goes — we fire small, check every piece by hand, and keep what does not pass out of the shop rather than discounting it.

What began as a way to furnish one apartment turned into standing orders from six workshops across the region, each run by people we know by name.

How we work

Three things we refuse to do.

Not a manifesto — just the constraints that keep the objects honest.

No. 01

No mass production

Every run is capped at what one workshop can finish by hand in a season. It means we sell out often. It also means nothing is made by a machine pretending to be a person.

No. 02

No middle layer

We buy directly from the makers. Nothing passes through a distributor, which is why the price on the page reflects the work rather than a chain of markups.

No. 03

No forced seasons

We do not redesign the collection twice a year to create urgency. Pieces stay until the workshop stops making them, and then they are simply gone.

The workshops

Who actually makes this.

Six workshops, all family-run, all paid per piece at a rate agreed before the run begins.

Ceramics — a family kiln outside Yogyakarta
Two brothers and their father, throwing stoneware on kick wheels. They have fired our carafes and bowls since the first batch, and they still send photographs of each glaze test.
Textiles — a four-shaft loom workshop in Central Java
Eleven weavers working on hand looms, producing the linen-cotton throws. The stonewashing happens in the same building, twice, which is why the hand is soft from the first wash.
Lighting — a woodturner and a pleater, working separately
Bases are turned from reclaimed oak by a single woodturner; shades are hand-pleated around brass frames by a two-person studio nearby. They have never met, but every lamp depends on both.
Wellness — a small pour studio
Coconut-soy wax poured in batches of sixty, into stoneware vessels from the same kiln that makes our tableware. The vessels are meant to be kept.
